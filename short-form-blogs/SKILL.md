# GMGM News Short-Form Blog Skill

Short-form posts are the domain authority engine. They win long-tail traffic
fast, support skyscraper cluster anchors, and test angles before a full
skyscraper investment. Every post must earn its place with a clear ranking
thesis, a differentiated angle, and verified facts.

---

## Site Context

- **Publication**: GMGM News (new domain, building authority)
- **Audience**: Retail investors, crypto enthusiasts, Web3 builders, fintech
  and payments professionals, tech-savvy general readers
- **Content pillars**: Stablecoins & payments | Cryptocurrency | Blockchain |
  Regulation & policy | Tech & VC (standalone)
- **SEO goal**: Rank fast on long-tail informational keywords within 30-60 days

---

## Workflow

Run every step in order. Do not skip the outline approval gate or the
fact-check rounds.

### Step 1: Keyword Validation

Run the GMGM keyword research skill before writing anything.

Confirm:
- Primary keyword has realistic ranking potential for a new domain (🟢 LOW
  or 🟡 MEDIUM competition)
- Search intent is informational
- A 600-1,200 word post is the right format (single specific long-tail, not
  a cluster that warrants a skyscraper)

If the keyword is 🔴 HIGH competition, do not proceed with a short post.
Escalate to a skyscraper or find a narrower long-tail variant.

### Step 2: SERP Gap Analysis

Research the top 15 ranking pages for the primary keyword before drafting.
The gap, not the keyword, determines the angle.

**Process:**

1. Identify the top 10-15 ranking URLs for the primary keyword.
2. For each result, note: the main angle, key facts covered, what is missing
   or underexplored.
3. Compile a gap table:

| Source | Angle | What It Misses |
|---|---|---|
| [publication] | [their framing] | [what GMGM can own] |

4. Identify the single most valuable uncovered angle. That is the article.

**For VC-backed, tech-adjacent, or Silicon Valley-funded events and companies:**
The gap in mainstream coverage is almost always the business model or investor
thesis. Sports, entertainment, and lifestyle media cover the spectacle.
Nobody clearly explains why the money is there and what it is actually buying.
Check this angle first before defaulting to the obvious explainer.

**Gap signals to hunt for beyond thin content:**
- Missing business model explanation (what does the company actually sell?)
- Missing investor thesis (why would rational people fund this?)
- Missing regulatory connection (what policy shift enables or threatens this?)
- Missing "control" or counter-narrative detail (e.g., athletes competing
  clean inside an "enhanced" competition)
- Missing structural concern (financial pressure on participants, coercion
  risk, power asymmetry)

### Step 3: Select the Angle and Target Keyword

State the contrarian thesis in one sentence before writing the outline.
If you cannot state it in one sentence, the angle is not sharp enough.

Examples of strong single-sentence theses:
- "The Enhanced Games is not a sports event; it is a $1.2 billion DTC
  pharma product launch using athletes as a marketing channel."
- "The GENIUS Act is not a crypto bill; it is the first federal framework
  that makes stablecoins a regulated payment instrument."

Primary keyword placement rules:
- Primary keyword must appear in the first 100 words of the article body.
- Primary keyword must appear naturally in at least one H2 heading.
- Do not force secondary keywords; let them appear in context.

### Step 4: Build the Outline

Structure for 600-1,200 words:

```
Headline (SEO-optimized, contrarian or curiosity-driven)
Dek (1-2 sentences; states the angle explicitly)
Intro (100-150 words; hook + thesis, no preamble)
H2: [Core explainer section] (150-200 words)
H2: [Business/mechanism/investor section] (200-250 words)
H2: [Counterargument or risk section] (150-200 words)
H2: [Optional: How to / practical details] (100-150 words)
Closing paragraph (60-80 words; restate the real stakes)
```

Rules:
- Maximum 5 H2 sections. More than 5 feels choppy at this length.
- No Table of Contents. Not needed under 1,200 words.
- No numbered H2 headings. Numbers belong in TOC lists, not headings.
- No introduction that restates the headline. Open with the hook directly.

**Present the outline to the user and get approval before drafting.**

### Step 5: Draft the Article

Write to the approved outline. Do not expand scope mid-draft.

Copy style rules:
- No em dashes anywhere. Rewrite with commas, colons, or split sentences.
- No double hyphens as punctuation.
- No "According to X", "per X", or "As per X" attribution. Use inline
  hyperlinks where the stat itself is the anchor text.
- Open with a direct declarative sentence or a scene-setting hook. Not a
  question. Not "In today's world..."
- Every specific number, percentage, dollar amount, date, and named
  statistic must be flagged for verification in the next step.
- Do not add financial, investment, or health advice. Add the appropriate
  disclaimer at the bottom if the topic touches drugs, supplements, trading,
  yield, or medical claims.

### Step 6: Two-Round Fact-Check

Run two separate fact-check passes. Do not collapse into one.

**Round 1: Core claims**

Verify every:
- Dollar figure (valuations, prize pools, market caps, stock prices)
- Named statistic or percentage (PED usage rates, market share figures)
- Direct quote (trace to the primary source, not a secondary summary)
- Investor or named party (full list, correct round attribution)
- Stock exchange, ticker, and listing date (company PRNewswire, not press
  summaries, which routinely get exchange names wrong)
- Athlete or participant details (check against official roster and
  independent reporting)
- Regulatory detail (exact agency, exact rule, exact date)

**Round 2: Logistics and event details**

For any article covering a live event, product launch, or time-sensitive
announcement, separately verify:
- Event date, time, and venue
- Streaming platforms and start times (opening vs. main card)
- Schedule of specific competitions or sessions
- Ticket or access information (free vs. paid, invite-only, subscription)
- Any post-event programming (concerts, side events)

These details come from different sources than the core claims and are
frequently wrong in early coverage. Always check the official event site
and official press releases in addition to media summaries.

**Quote verification rule:**
If a quote appears in a secondary article or summary, do not use it as a
direct quote until you have confirmed the exact wording against the primary
source (press release, shareholder letter, official interview transcript,
or video). If the primary source is unavailable, paraphrase and link to
the secondary source without quotation marks.

**Athlete count rule:**
Official event press releases, not media coverage, carry the authoritative
figure. Counts in media summaries frequently vary by 2-5 athletes as the
roster changes close to the event.

### Step 7: Add Inline Hyperlinks

Link to primary sources only. If the stat lives in a company press release,
link to the press release. If a reporter did original research, link to
their article.

Rules:
- The statistic or claim itself is the anchor text, not "click here" or
  "source."
- Do not link every sentence. Link facts that are specific, verifiable, and
  useful for a reader who wants to go deeper.
- Only embed a body link if the target page exists and has a live URL.
  List planned internal links in the metadata block; embed them in the body
  only after the target page is published.
- UTM format for internal links:
  `utm_source=gmgm&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign={this-slug}&utm_content={linked-slug}`

### Step 8: Write Metadata

```
Meta title: [Primary keyword + angle hook, under 60 characters]
Meta description: [News hook + contrarian angle, under 160 characters exactly]
Primary keyword: [one phrase]
Secondary keywords: [3-6 phrases, comma-separated]
Suggested slug: [primary-keyword-hyphenated]
Internal links to add: [list of target pages, even if not yet published]
External sources used: [source list for editor reference]
```

Meta description rule: state the news hook first, then the surprising angle.
"The Enhanced Games launch today in Las Vegas with PEDs welcome. But the real
story is the $1.2 billion peptide business Peter Thiel is building around it."

### Step 9: Publish to Notion

Publish as a standalone workspace-level page (no database parent), matching
the structure of existing GMGM pages.

Page structure order:
1. Pink metadata block (`<span color="pink_bg">`) with all metadata fields
2. `---` divider
3. Article body (intro, H2 sections, closing paragraph)
4. `---` divider
5. Disclaimer (if topic touches finance, health, or medical claims)
6. Publish date

Use `<span color="pink_bg">**Field:**</span><span color="pink_bg"> Value</span>`
for every metadata field.

## Angle Detection Cheat Sheet

Use this when the topic is not obviously crypto or payments.

| Topic type | First gap to check |
|---|---|
| VC-backed event or competition | Business model: what does the company actually sell? |
| New regulation or law | Who benefits commercially, not just who complies |
| New AI or tech product launch | What existing market does this threaten or replace? |
| Celebrity or political investment | Why this asset class, why now, what is the investor thesis? |
| Sports or entertainment crossover | What is the financial structure underneath the spectacle? |
| Government action or enforcement | Which companies gain regulatory advantage? |

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- [ ] Primary keyword appears in the first 100 words and in at least one H2
- [ ] Meta description is 160 characters or fewer
- [ ] No em dashes anywhere in copy or metadata
- [ ] Every direct quote traced to a primary source
- [ ] Dollar figures, percentages, and named statistics verified in Round 1
- [ ] Event times, platforms, and logistics verified in Round 2 (if applicable)
- [ ] Inline links go to primary sources
- [ ] No body links to unpublished pages
- [ ] Internal links listed in metadata block

     medical claims

## Accumulated Lessons

*This section grows as GMGM News publishes and collects ranking data. Each
lesson represents a real calibration from an article produced with this skill.*

**Enhanced Games (May 24, 2026)**
- Mainstream sports and entertainment media flood the zone on the obvious
  story (doping controversy). The business model angle was uncovered in
  mainstream outlets and ranked as the differentiating thesis.
- Stock exchange names are frequently wrong in early media coverage. The
  Enhanced Group SPAC was listed on Nasdaq (as APAD) but the resulting
  company (ENHA) listed on NYSE. All coverage initially said Nasdaq. Always
  verify against the company's own PRNewswire announcement.
- Athlete counts in early coverage varied between 40 and 42. The official
  Resorts World press release carried the authoritative figure (40).
- A quote summarized in Gizmodo from the CEO could not be verified against
  the primary shareholder letter wording. Replaced with a paraphrase linked
  to the shareholder letter. Never carry forward a quote from a secondary
  summary without confirming exact wording.
- The "non-enhanced control athletes" detail (Kerley, Armstrong, Evelyn
  competing clean) was in primary sources but missed by most articles. It
  added genuine texture and reframed the "Enhanced Games" branding as
  internally contradictory.
- Event logistics (stream times, platforms, opening vs. main card split)
  came from the official event site and Roku PRNewswire, not from sports
  media coverage, which had inconsistencies. Always check official sources
  for logistics in Round 2.
