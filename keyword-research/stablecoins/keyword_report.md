## Keywords to Avoid (for now)

**1. "what is a stablecoin"**
Wikipedia owns position 1. CoinDesk, Chainlink, Chainalysis, Bank of England, and World Economic Forum fill the next spots with comprehensive, well-linked guides. Total results exceed 1 million. A new domain posting a generic "what is stablecoin" article has essentially zero chance of cracking the top 20 before the 90-day mark. Wait until the beginner's guide (#1 in the content mix) has aged and accumulated backlinks, then consider a tighter variant.

**2. "USDC stablecoin" (standalone)**
Circle's own domain (usdc.com and circle.com), Wikipedia, Gemini, Coinbase, Kraken, and MoonPay all have heavily optimized, high-authority pages. The brand itself controls multiple top results. USDC coverage belongs inside the USDT vs USDC comparison skyscraper rather than as a standalone target.

**3. "best stablecoins 2026"**
Bitget, KuCoin, and exchange platforms dominate with listicles that have strong trust signals from their own user bases and massive domain authority. Commercial-intent queries like "best" are disproportionately hard for new sites with no link profile. Revisit after 6 months.

**4. "stablecoin yield" (generic)**
CoinTelegraph has multiple posts. Stripe has two dedicated guides. Ledn, Aave, and Coinbase landing pages also appear. The query is too broad and too tied to product/exchange domains that will always outrank independent editorial for this term. The targeted angle — "how to earn interest on stablecoins + risks in 2026" — is a much better lane and is in the content mix above.

**5. "Tether USDT"**
Tether's own domain, CoinMarketCap, Wikipedia, CoinDesk, and Investopedia all rank with deeply established, constantly updated pages. Investopedia's Tether guide alone is one of the most-linked stablecoin pages on the web. This is a flagship keyword for the category — approachable only once GMGM News has domain authority built up from the cluster strategy above.
